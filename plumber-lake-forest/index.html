Selling a Lake Forest home? The sewer inspection comes first

The city has its own rule here, and it catches plumbing problems on the way to closing.

A city ordinance requires a Home Sewer Inspection when water billing is transferred on the sale of a single-family home or duplex. The inspector checks downspouts, sump pumps and yard drains for connections to the sanitary sewer that should not be there. The city charges a set fee, a lower fee for re-inspection, and schedules the visit within 10 working days. Permits are needed before any correction work.

A sump pump that discharges into a floor drain is the classic failure, and it is a plumbing fix, not a paperwork one. We reroute the discharge, test the pump and check the lateral with a camera from our sewer camera inspection service so the repair list is complete before the inspector arrives.

Closing date close? Tell dispatch and we will find an early appointment when one is open.

An old core around a much newer ring

Lake Forest has 7,282 housing units in the latest Census estimate. About 18 percent, 1,310 homes, date to 1939 or earlier, and about 31 percent went up before 1960. Yet the median build year is 1976, because roughly 2,900 homes were added from 1980 to 2009. The result is two very different plumbing stocks in one city.

Ravines, bluffs and four inches in four hours

In the July 2017 storm, Lake Forest saw about four inches of rain in four hours, which the county estimated as roughly a 70-year event. The city says heavy stormwater can overload the sanitary sewer and push wastewater back into homes, and it names flushable wipes as a cause of blockages. It also recommends a battery-powered backup sump pump.

The city's ravine pages describe slope drains and retaining walls at Forest Park and restoration work at Seminary Ravine, so homes near the bluffs deal with moving water above and below the foundation. For repeated basement flooding the city offers a free consultation through Public Works, and we can handle the plumbing side through basement waterproofing once you know the cause.

5

Lead service lines: two estimates, one answer

Lake Forest states that its treated water and its roughly 168 miles of water mains carry no lead. Privately owned service lines are another matter, and the city's own figures vary. Its Water and Sewer page estimates about 400 properties may have a private lead service line, while an older FAQ said about 300 homes.

Either way, the homeowner owns the line. A permit is needed to replace it, and the city waives the permit fee. If you want to know what is coming into your basement, our water service line repair team can identify the material and quote the replacement.

Lake Forest facts for your records

Local note

City systems behind the plumbing

Lake Forest runs its own Lake Michigan water plant, drawing from 42-inch and 24-inch intake pipelines, and it keeps separate sanitary and storm sewer systems. Sanitary flow goes to a treatment plant on Clavey Road operated by the North Shore Sanitary District, which bills treatment separately from the city's sewer charge. The city had 19,367 residents in the 2020 Census across 17.2 square miles.

The plumbing code the city adopted is the Illinois State Plumbing Code as modified by Chapter 150 of the City Code. Permit applications for plumbing, water heaters and sewers are filed with Community Development at City Hall, 220 East Deerpath. Ask them if your job needs one. Next door, Lake Bluff and Libertyville are on our dispatch map too.
