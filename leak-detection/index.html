Emergency Leak Detection in North Chicago, IL

A water meter that keeps spinning with every tap closed, a stain spreading across a ceiling, or a warm patch on the floor means water is escaping somewhere you cannot see. North Chicago Express Plumbing responds 24/7. Until we arrive, shut off the main valve if water is actively soaking a room, and move belongings out of the wet area.

We find the leak first, then tell you what it will take to fix it. No walls get opened until we know exactly where the water is.

1

The meter test you can do before we arrive

It takes a few minutes and tells us a lot.

  1. 1

    Turn everything off

    Close every tap, stop the dishwasher and washing machine, and make sure no toilet is running.

  2. 2

    Read the meter

    Find the small dial or triangle on the meter face. If it moves, water is flowing somewhere.

  3. 3

    Close the main valve

    Shut the valve where the line enters the house, then check the meter again.

  4. 4

    Read the result

    If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house.

This one test sorts most calls into inside or outside, which decides the tools we bring.

2

Water bill climbing but nothing looks wet

A slow rise in your bill across a few cycles is often a leak that started small. Running toilets, dripping valves under a vanity, and a leaking water heater connection show up this way. Hidden pipe leaks in a wall or under a floor can run for weeks with little visible damage.

Check your own bill against the same season last year, and note whether anything changed in the house. If a property has been sitting empty or just changed tenants, a leak is more likely than a change in habits. Bring the last two bills and we will compare.

3

Ceiling, wall, and attic leaks

Stains on a ceiling can come from a supply line, a drain line, a roof leak, or condensation on a cold pipe. A drain leak often leaves a brownish ring and shows up when the fixture above is used. A supply leak keeps going when no fixture is running.

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to trace the wet area back toward its source, and we often locate it without cutting much drywall. When the water comes from a roof or window, we say so and you will need a roofer instead. We would rather tell you that now than open a wall for nothing.

4

Under the house, in the crawl space, and under the slab

Warm spots on a floor, a faint hissing sound at night, or damp soil in a crawl space suggest a leak below the living space. We use acoustic listening gear and pressure testing to isolate which line is losing water, then narrow it to a few feet.

For slab leaks, we pinpoint the spot before any concrete is touched. Then you decide between a spot repair, a reroute through the walls, or a larger repiping plan. That decision stays with you, backed by what we found.

5

Leaks between the meter and the house

North Chicago's lead service line plan counted 5,653 service lines in the city's water system, with 849 identified as lead and 144 galvanized lines flagged for replacement. Older service lines are a common place for a hidden leak, and the line is usually buried and out of sight. Wet ground in the yard, a soft spot in the lawn, or low pressure at every tap can be signs.

Before anyone digs, call 811 so JULIE can mark buried utilities. Ask the city water department whether the problem sits on its side of the line or yours. If the leak is on your side, our water service line repair page explains what comes next. Homes on the Navy's own system are a different case, so start with your housing provider.

6

Irrigation, pools, and outdoor spigots

Sprinkler zones, hose bibs, and pool lines leak out of sight and can run up a bill during summer months. We can test whether a lawn system or pool line is losing water and mark the area. Water bills that jump only in warm months often point outside.

Checking for a leak before you buy or rent out a place

Landlords and buyers often ask for a leak check when a unit changes hands. In a city where about two-thirds of households rent, a quiet leak in a vacant apartment can run for weeks. We test supply lines, look at the water heater and fixtures, and write down what we found.
