Emergency Water Service Line Repair in North Chicago, IL

When the pipe between the street and your meter breaks, water is either gone or pouring out of the ground. We answer 24/7. Shut the main valve inside the house, stop using fixtures, and note whether the yard is wet or the meter dial is still turning. That tells us a lot before we arrive.

We repair and replace the buried water service line, find leaks on it, and tell you what the city has published about lead and galvanized lines in North Chicago. We call JULIE before any dig and keep you informed of what the work will touch.

1

Line broken or leaking: what to do in the first hour

  1. 1

    Close the main valve

    Find the valve where the water line enters the building, normally beside the meter. Turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, do not force it.

  2. 2

    Check the meter

    With every fixture off, look at the dial or small triangle on the meter. If it still moves, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures, or before it.

  3. 3

    Look at the ground

    Soft soil, a sunken patch or water bubbling near the foundation points to a buried leak. Photograph it.

  4. 4

    Call the right people

    Call us for the pipe on your property. If you think the break is in the street or at the connection to the main, the city water department's after-hours line is 847-596-8774.

Do not start digging yourself. Call JULIE at 811 before any digging, because a service line may sit close to gas, electric or communications lines.

2

Signs the buried line is failing before it breaks

  • ✓Pressure drops at every fixture. Low pressure through the whole house points to the line, a partly closed main valve, or corrosion narrowing the pipe. One faucet alone is usually a local clog.
  • ✓A water bill climbs with no change in use. A slow buried leak can run for weeks before anything shows on the surface.
  • ✓Wet or unusually green spots in the yard. Soil that stays soft in dry weather is worth a look.
  • ✓Discolored water after the line is disturbed. Rust-colored or cloudy water points to old steel or galvanized pipe shedding scale.
  • ✓Meter moves with everything off. The simplest test for any leak, and one we use on every service line call.

If you cannot tell whether the leak is on the service line or inside the house, a trace helps. Our leak detection team can locate a buried leak without digging a trench to find it, and tell you whether it is on this line.

3

North Chicago's lead line count, in the city's own numbers

Local note

What the city's 2024 plan says

The lead service line plan the city filed on September 1, 2024 counts 5,653 service lines in all. Of those, 849 are lead, 144 are galvanized and need replacement, and 4,660 are not lead. The plan's table shows no lines in an unknown, suspected-lead category.

The city's 2023 water quality report shows a lead 90th percentile of 15 parts per billion, the same number as the federal action level, with 3 sample sites over it and no violation recorded. A lead line does not mean the water at your tap is unsafe on its own, but it is a reason to ask the city about testing and to use a filter certified for lead until the line is checked.

We cannot tell you your line's material from the street. We can look at the pipe where it enters your basement or utility space and tell you what it appears to be. A magnet, a scratch test and the color help, and you can confirm against the city's inventory.

4

The city's free replacement phase, and who is in it

Next door in Waukegan the city runs its own separate lead program. Here, North Chicago is replacing lead service lines at no cost to property owners in one defined area, bounded by Green Bay Road on the west, Argonne Road to the north and Broadway Avenue to the south. This phase covers about 175 properties and is paid for by an Illinois EPA grant. Owners in the area must sign an access form before crews can work.

If your address is inside that boundary, call the city water department before you hire anyone. The city runs that program, we do not, and you should not pay for work the program covers. If your home is outside it, a replacement is a private job. We can quote it, show you what the work will involve, and name the price before any digging.

Naval property takes up a big share of the city and runs on the Navy's own water system, so the city's inventory does not cover those homes. If you live in Navy housing, ask your housing office, not the city.

How a service line job runs

  1. 1

    Locate the line and call JULIE

    Utility marking comes before any excavation. We tell you what dig timing that adds.

  2. 2

    Ask about permits

    The city building department, 847-596-8650, tells us what applies to your address. We do not assume.

  3. 3

    Expose, repair or replace

    We open only what we need and keep your yard, driveway or sidewalk in view before we touch them.

  4. 4

    Test and restore

    We pressure test the new pipe, flush the line, and tell you what to restore on the surface.

Plumbers in Illinois are licensed by the state's public health department. We are licensed and insured, and proof is yours for the asking.
