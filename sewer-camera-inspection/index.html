Sewer Camera Inspection in North Chicago, IL

A camera on a flexible cable lets us see inside your sewer line instead of guessing. We use it to find roots, breaks, sags, belly spots and buildups in North Chicago homes, rentals and small buildings, then explain what we saw in plain words.

Most inspections are planned, and we offer same-day appointments when available. If sewage is already coming up in a floor drain, stop using water in the building and call. Urgent backups are handled 24/7.

1

When a camera beats a guess

Plenty of sewer problems look alike from the outside. A slow main drain, a gurgle after you flush, a sewer smell in one bathroom and a wet patch in the yard (which might also be a water supply leak, so ask about leak detection) can all trace back to different faults. Cleaning the line may clear the symptom for a week and leave the cause.

A camera run answers the question before money goes into digging or relining. It shows whether the pipe is cracked, offset, root-filled, bellied or just greasy. We treat it as a way to decide, and sometimes it tells you the line is fine.

Good reasons to book a camera run

  • ✓Repeated clogs in the main drain after cleaning
  • ✓A sewer smell that comes and goes with no dry trap to blame
  • ✓Soggy or sunken ground along the line from the house to the street
  • ✓Buying a house and wanting to know what is under the lawn
  • ✓A past repair you want checked
2

What happens during the inspection

  1. 1

    Find the way in

    We look for a cleanout near the foundation or a pulled toilet as the entry point. No cleanout means a bigger job, and we tell you that first.

  2. 2

    Feed the camera

    The camera travels down the line while a screen shows live video and a footage counter. We stop at anything that looks wrong.

  3. 3

    Locate the problem

    A transmitter in the camera head lets us trace the spot above ground, so you know how deep and where it sits.

  4. 4

    Review it together

    You see the images, hear what we found and get options with a clear price before work starts.

3

Why age matters under North Chicago lawns

About 32 percent of North Chicago's housing was built before 1960, and nearly 19 percent before 1940. Older service lines can be clay tile, cast iron or early plastic that has shifted, and tree roots find joints in these materials easily. We do not assume anything until we have seen it on camera.

Newer homes are not exempt. The median year built here is 1973, which still puts many lines past the half-century mark. Builders' backfill settles and pipes sag, so low spots collect waste and then clog.

4

Which sewer: ours, the street, or the district

Local note

Who is responsible for what

North Chicago's sewage is handled by the North Shore Water Reclamation District, which says the sanitary and storm sewers in its service area are separate systems with no combined sewers. A camera run helps you find out which side of that line a problem sits on.

The city has its own sanitary sewer cleaning and televising program for its mains. Your private lateral, the pipe from the house to the connection, is generally the property owner's concern, but who owns the local collector sewers is not clear in the city's public material. Ask the city before you pay for work out in the street.

5

After the footage: your options

If the camera finds roots, a sewer line cleaning visit may restore flow, and we can re-inspect to check the pipe wall. If it finds a break or collapse, the fix is a sewer line repair, which may be a short spot dig or a longer replacement.

Before any digging, we call JULIE at 811 so underground utilities are marked. Private sewer work can also need approval from the city, so we ask about that before we start rather than after.
