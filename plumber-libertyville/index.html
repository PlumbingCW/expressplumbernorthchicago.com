Twelve drainage areas the village has already named

Libertyville publishes where its storm sewers struggle, and that list is a good map of basement risk.

The Village's Master Stormwater Plan from 2019 names these study areas. They are: Appley-Second Avenue, Carriage Hill, Copeland Manor, East and West Ellis, Harding and Willow, the Highlands Subdivision near Dowden Park, Interlaken Lane, Lange and Cook, Liberty Bell and Fourth, the Rockland Road area, Stonegate Road and the Winchester Road area. The plan says localized flooding usually follows rain above two inches and that many storm sewers were built before modern stormwater standards.

Lake County's flood plan lists 45 flood problem areas in Libertyville affecting 530 properties as of July 2017, and the Village's own FAQ mentions areas where sewer backups may occur. In those pockets a slow floor drain after heavy rain is a warning, not a nuisance. Our drain cleaning and camera work finds out whether the restriction is in your line or downstream.

The 1970s built a lot of this village

The median Libertyville home dates to 1976. Census figures count 1,967 homes built from 1970 to 1979 and another 1,405 from 1980 to 1989, so roughly 3,400 of the village's 7,925 units are now 35 to 55 years old. That is the age where original water heaters, supply shutoffs and the first replacement tanks are all running out at once.

The oldest blocks and lead water lines

The Village's lead page says lead water lines were commonly installed before World War II, and about 740 Libertyville homes date to 1939 or earlier. The Village has built a water service material inventory with an online dashboard, prepared a lead service line replacement plan dated April 2026 and offers a voluntary cost-sharing replacement program.

If your house is in the oldest group, we check where the line enters and what it is made of. Replacing the full run from the main to the meter is a job for our water service line repair crew, and it pairs well with any interior work you were already planning.

A village that treats its own sewage

Libertyville runs three utilities: water, sanitary sewer and storm sewer. Its wastewater plant has a design capacity of 4.0 million gallons a day and discharges treated water to the Des Plaines River. Water comes from Lake Michigan through the supply agency known as CLCJAWA, which Libertyville began using in May 1992. A few wells remain as an emergency source.

That structure means a backup in the street main is the Village's to fix, while anything inside your property line is yours. Because storm and sanitary lines are separate here, a downspout or sump pump wired into the sanitary side is a repair we flag during any visit.

Paperwork a Libertyville plumber has to show

The Village does not register contractors, but it asks to see the State of Illinois Plumbing Contractor Registration, the plumber's license and a signed Letter of Intent for each project. Owner-performed residential plumbing needs a Plumbing Verification Letter. Private sewer repairs can be permitted through an online application. The Building Division is at 325 N. Milwaukee Avenue, Suite 203.

What the Building Division usually asks to see

  • ✓A copy of the state plumbing contractor registration.
  • ✓The plumber's Illinois license, which anyone can verify through the state lookup.
  • ✓A signed Letter of Intent describing the project.
  • ✓For sewer repair, an online permit application.

The Village adopted the 2014 Illinois Plumbing Code, effective May 1, 2019, with a local WaterSense requirement for new fixtures. That matters when we do plumbing installation work such as a new bath. We cannot tell you from the Village's pages whether a heater swap needs a permit, so we ask the Building Division first.

What makes this village different

Libertyville had 20,579 residents in the 2020 Census over 8.81 square miles. Its Downtown Libertyville Historic District, nominated to the National Register, covers parts of ten blocks and includes 71 resources, 49 of them contributing, so the older commercial blocks sit at the center of the village.

Metra's Libertyville station is at 200 W. Lake Street, and Advocate Condell Medical Center is at 801 S. Milwaukee Avenue, both in 60048. Calls also reach us from Gurnee and Lake Forest, and our crews cover them from the same base.
