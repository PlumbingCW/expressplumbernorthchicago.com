Water Heater Tank Installation Cost in North Chicago, IL

Water heater tank installation cost in North Chicago depends on a short list of choices: the fuel, the tank size, how much of the old setup has to change, and the permit. The tank itself is only one line on the bill. Venting, gas or electric connections, code upgrades and haul-away usually decide whether a job is simple or not.

This page walks through each price driver in the order a plumber thinks about it, so you can read a quote line by line. We do not print dollar amounts here because a number without a look at your basement or closet would be a guess.

1

Start with what is already there

The cheapest installs are like-for-like swaps: same fuel, same size, same location, and the connections line up. The tank slides out, the new one goes in, and the lines reconnect. Anything that breaks that pattern adds labor or parts.

Before you ask for a price, note the fuel (gas or electric), the tank size on the label, where the heater sits, and what is around it. A photo of the label and the venting is the most helpful thing you can send. It lets us flag problems before the visit, not after the old tank is already drained.

2

The six things that move the price

What changes the price

Every quote for a tank heater is some mix of these factors. None of them is a trick. They are the real work.

Fuel typeGas units need a vent path and a gas connection. Electric units need the right breaker and wire size. Switching fuel adds work, and usually a lot of it.
Tank size and efficiencyA larger tank or a higher efficiency model costs more up front. A tank sized to your household avoids both running out of hot water and paying to heat water you do not use.
VentingA gas heater's flue must be sized and routed correctly. Old, rusted or undersized vent pipe often has to be replaced, which adds parts and time.
Access and locationA heater in an open basement is quick. One in an attic, a tight closet or a second-floor unit takes more labor, and stairs matter.
Code and safety partsExpansion tanks, a pan with a drain, new shutoff valves, a pressure relief valve and discharge pipe may be required or advisable, and each adds to the total.
Permit and haul-awayA permit may be required in your town, and the old tank has to leave the house. Some quotes bundle these and some list them separately, so read the line items.
If a failed tank damaged your home, ask your insurer what it covers. We can give you a written scope and photos for the claim.

4

Tank or tankless: the cost question behind the cost question

A tank costs less to buy and install, takes more floor space, and stores hot water that cools down. A tankless unit costs more up front and often needs bigger gas lines or electrical service, but it takes less room. Neither is a better choice in every home. The right answer depends on your household, your space and your fuel.

If you are not sure, tell us how many people live in the home and how many showers run at once. We would rather size the tank right than sell you the most expensive unit. If you are deciding between replacing a failed heater and installing a first one in a new space, see our notes on water heater installation and water heater replacement.

5

What install day looks like

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    Fuel or power is turned off, the cold supply is closed, and the old tank is drained to a floor drain or outdoors.

  2. 2

    Disconnect and remove

    Water lines, vent and fuel connections come off. The old tank is moved out.

  3. 3

    Set the new tank

    The new heater goes on a level base, with a drain pan where needed.

  4. 4

    Connect and vent

    We connect the water, then the gas or electric supply, and the vent. Every joint gets checked for leaks.

  5. 5

    Fill, test and explain

    We fill the tank before turning on fuel or power, light or energize it, check the flue draft on gas units, and show you the shutoffs.

Before We Arrive

1Clear the area
