How to Remove and Install a Toilet: North Chicago, IL Guide

Learning how to remove and install a toilet is one of the more doable plumbing jobs for a North Chicago, IL homeowner, because it runs on water only, with no gas and no electricity. It takes about two hours, a helper for the lifting, and a new wax ring. The trouble usually hides under the old toilet, in the flange and the floor, so this guide spends as much time on what you find there as on the swap itself.

If the floor is soft or the flange is cracked, stop and call us. We will tell you in the steps where that line is.

1

Gather these before you shut the water off

Parts and tools for a standard toilet swap

  • ✓New toilet. Match the rough-in, which is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the floor drain. Twelve inches is the most common, but measure yours.
  • ✓New wax ring or wax-free seal. Never reuse the old one. A used ring will not seal again.
  • ✓Closet bolts and a supply line. Buy new. Old bolts are often corroded, and an old supply line is a leak waiting to happen.
  • ✓Adjustable wrench, putty knife, bucket, sponge. The knife scrapes the old wax. The sponge and a small cup empty the last of the water.
  • ✓Rags, gloves, and a helper. A toilet bowl can weigh a lot, and porcelain slips when wet. Lift with your legs and with another person.

Lay down a towel or flattened box to protect the floor. Have the new toilet unboxed and checked for chips before you remove the old one, so you are not left with a hole and a damaged replacement.

2

Removing the old toilet, one step at a time

  1. 1

    Shut off and drain

    Close the supply valve on the wall by turning it clockwise. Flush to empty the tank, then sponge out what remains in the tank and bowl.

  2. 2

    Disconnect the supply line

    Unscrew the nut at the bottom of the tank by hand or with a wrench. Keep a towel under it, because a little water will come out.

  3. 3

    Remove the bolt caps and nuts

    Pop the caps off the base. Loosen the nuts on both sides. If they spin, hold the bolt with pliers. If they are rusted solid, a hacksaw blade slipped under the nut will cut them.

  4. 4

    Rock and lift

    Gently rock the bowl to break the wax seal, then lift straight up and set it on towels. Carry it out upright if you can, because waste water still sits in the trap.

  5. 5

    Plug the opening

    Stuff a rag into the drain flange right away. It blocks sewer gas and keeps tools and wax from falling into the pipe.

  6. 6

    Scrape the flange clean

    Remove the old wax with a putty knife. Wipe the surface so the new ring sits on clean metal or plastic.

3

The flange check that decides everything

With the toilet out, look at the flange, the ring that connects the toilet to the drain and holds the bolts. It should be solid, level with or slightly above the finished floor, and firmly fastened. A cracked, bent, or loose flange will let the new toilet rock and leak no matter how well you tighten the bolts.

North Chicago has a mixed housing stock. The Census puts the median home at 1973, but 32.3 percent of homes were built before 1960. In older homes a cast iron drain, a repaired flange, or a subfloor stained by years of slow leaks is common. If you see dark, spongy wood or crumbling edges, do not set the new toilet on top of it.

4

Setting the new toilet

  1. 1

    Fit the wax ring and bolts

    Set new closet bolts in the flange slots. Place the wax ring on the flange or on the horn of the toilet, but not both.

  2. 2

    Lower the bowl straight down

    Line the bolt holes up and lower the toilet in one motion. Do not slide it after it touches the wax. Press down with your body weight to seat it.

  3. 3

    Tighten in small turns

    Alternate between the two nuts. Stop when the toilet no longer rocks. Cranking too hard cracks the porcelain.

  4. 4

    Attach the tank if it is separate

    Follow the maker's order for bolts and rubber washers. Snug them evenly, because the tank is also china.

  5. 5

    Connect the supply, turn on the water, and test

    Open the valve slowly. Let the tank fill, flush several times, and check every joint with dry tissue for the first sign of a drip.

Trim the bolts, snap on the caps, and run a thin bead of caulk around the base if you like. A rocking toilet after tightening points to an uneven floor, which a plumber can fix with shims.

5

When to hand the job to a plumber

Call us if the flange is damaged, if the floor is soft, if the shutoff valve will not close, or if the toilet will not seat flat. Call us as well if you are moving the toilet to a new spot, because that changes the drain and the vent and may require a permit. For new fixture work, our plumbing installation crew handles the piping and the rough-in.

Overflowing right now? Close the supply valve behind the toilet, lift the tank lid, and push the flapper down to stop the flow. Then call. We are open 24/7.

Waukegan and Libertyville: the local rules to ask about

Local note

What Waukegan and Libertyville publish about plumbing work

Waukegan's plumbing page says that permits are required for all plumbing revisions and for replacing hot water heaters, and that contractors must be registered with the city. The page does not say whether a like-for-like toilet swap counts, so phone the building department before you start, especially if you plan to move the fixture or hire a plumber.

Libertyville's municipal code adopts the Illinois Plumbing Code, and Section 6-191 sets a local WaterSense requirement for new fixtures. Check the label on any toilet you buy for a Libertyville address. The village also requires a Plumbing Verification Letter for owner-performed residential plumbing, so a do-it-yourself install there is not paperwork-free. The Building Division can explain what applies to your project.
