Broken Sewer Line Repair for North Chicago, IL Homes

A broken sewer line sends waste water into the soil instead of out to the street, and the first signs are easy to mistake for a stubborn clog. If drains gurgle, several fixtures back up together, or a wet patch keeps appearing in the yard, the problem may be a broken sewer line, and North Chicago, IL homes of every age can have one.

This guide shows how to tell a break from a clog, how a camera inspection pins down the damage, what repair choices exist, and which permit rules apply in Waukegan and Lake Forest. If sewage is backing up into the house right now, stop using water and call.

1

Signs that point to a break and not a clog

Warning signs worth taking seriously

  • ✓More than one drain acts up. A clog in one sink stays in that sink. When the tub gurgles as the washer drains, the shared line is the suspect.
  • ✓Backups keep returning after cleaning. A line that is cleared and then fails again within weeks often has a crack, a belly, or a root intrusion that cleaning does not cure.
  • ✓A soggy or sunken spot in the yard. Waste water escaping near the line can soften soil, green up the grass, or cause a dip over the pipe.
  • ✓A sewer smell outside. Odor near a cleanout, a basement floor drain, or a patch of lawn can come from a broken pipe.
  • ✓Slow drains in the lowest bathroom. The lowest fixture feels trouble first, because it is closest to a blockage or break.

None of these proves a break by itself. Together they tell a plumber where to look. The next step is a camera, not a shovel.

2

Why sewer lines fail

Age and material

Older homes may have clay, cast iron, or fiber pipe that cracks, flakes, or separates at the joints over many decades.

Roots

Roots find tiny gaps at joints, grow into the pipe, and push the seam wider. The pipe still carries flow until the roots catch debris.

Ground movement and loads

Frost, heavy vehicles, and settling soil bend pipe and open joints. A low spot, called a belly, then holds waste.

In North Chicago, the median home was built in 1973, but 32.3 percent were built before 1960, according to the Census. A pre-1960 house is the one where we expect older pipe material to show up on camera.

The city's sewers are provided by the North Shore Water Reclamation District, which states that sanitary and storm sewers in its service area are separate systems, so rain should not be entering your sanitary line through the street. Cracks in your own lateral can still let groundwater in.

3

How a camera inspection finds the break

A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable goes into the line through a cleanout or a removed toilet. The picture shows cracks, offset joints, roots, bellies, and where the pipe changes material. A locator on the surface marks the depth and position of any defect, so a repair can focus on a few feet instead of the whole run.

We recommend a recording before any repair is priced. It gives you proof of the condition and lets you compare repair options with facts. Our sewer camera inspection service is built for this, and the recording is yours to keep.

5

What to do while you wait for help

  1. 1

    Stop using water

    Every flush, shower, and load of laundry adds to the backup. Tell everyone in the home.

  2. 2

    Keep people and pets out of the wet area

    Sewage carries bacteria. Wear gloves and boots if you must walk through it, and keep children away.

  3. 3

    Turn off power to a flooded basement only if you can do so safely

    If water is near the panel or outlets, stay out and call your electric utility or an electrician.

  4. 4

    Do not pour chemicals down the drain

    Drain cleaner will not fix a broken pipe, and it makes the later work hazardous.

  5. 5

    Call us

    We answer around the clock. Tell us which fixtures are affected and what you see in the yard.

Take photos of the damage and list what was affected. If you plan to make an insurance claim, a record from the first hour is valuable.

6

Waukegan and Lake Forest: permits, flooding, and who maintains what

Local note

Sewer repair rules and flood history in Waukegan and Lake Forest

Waukegan's sewer page says permits are required for installing cleanouts, making sewer repairs, and doing replacement work on private sewer lines, and that the application goes in online before the work starts. Registered excavators with a bond may dig for discovery only, contractors pay an annual registration fee, an inspection is required before the work is covered, and fees double when work begins without a permit. The city collects sewage, and the North Shore Sanitary District treats it.

On flooding, the Lake County Lake Michigan watershed plan counts 106 flood problem areas affecting 185 properties in Waukegan in July 2017, the most of any listed municipality, and says more than half of the 2017 flood reports were basement flooding including sewer backups. It also notes sanitary sewer backups during the April 2013 rainstorm across the planning area.

Lake Forest states that it maintains two separate systems of sewers, sanitary and storm, and that sanitary wastewater goes to a treatment plant on Clavey Road operated by the North Shore Sanitary District. The city lists a sewer permit for sanitary or storm work and cites flushable wipes as one cause of sewer backups.
