A sewer backup in Lake Bluff: who to call first

The Village of Lake Bluff owns and cleans its own sanitary and storm sewers and the lift stations that serve them. Its published instruction is to contact Village Hall before you call a plumber when the sanitary sewer backs up, and to reach the Police Dispatch Center if it happens after hours. We think that is sound advice. If the blockage is in the street main, the Village clears it and our visit would not help.

If the Village finds its main clear, the problem is yours: a lateral, a floor drain, a collapsed section or roots. That is when you call us, day or night, and we start with a camera run so the repair matches what is actually in the pipe. Our sewer camera inspection work sits behind most of these calls.

Seven flood study areas and a lot of clay

The Village has mapped where its own residents see water.

The Village's 2019 Stormwater Master Plan names seven flood study areas: West Center Avenue, the Scranton Avenue viaduct, Moffett Road, Maple Avenue, Oak Terrace, West Terrace and Simpson Street. It was built on more than 300 resident responses. The plan also calls the soil predominantly high in clay, which is why water sits instead of soaking away.

The storm of record was July 12 and 13, 2017, with over 7 inches of rain in 12 hours, about a 130-year storm by the county's estimate. The Village's own advice for homes that take water is practical, and we install and service the plumbing side of it:

What the Village stormwater plan recommends

  • 1An overhead sewer, where a house can be converted.
  • 2A sanitary backflow valve on the lateral, if conversion costs too much.
  • 3A sump pump with battery backup, because the power often goes with the storm.

We handle the pump and valve work under sump pump repair, and we will tell you if the wet spot you are paying to fix is a grading problem in your yard.

Three kinds of Lake Bluff house

Older core and east side

Much of the village was platted in the 1920s, and the streets near the downtown include original camp meeting cottages. Older supply pipe and older sewer laterals are the first things we check.

Postwar Terraces

The Terrace neighborhoods are mostly postwar homes. Original drain and supply pipe from that era is where we start when a basement bathroom acts up.

Late 1980s and 1990s subdivisions

Tangley Oaks, Armour Woods, Belle Foret and the Sanctuary were built later. Water heaters and fixtures here are reaching replacement age.

The numbers back that split. The median home was built in 1980, about 22 percent date to before 1960 and about half to before 1980. Around 94 percent of the village's 2,386 housing units are detached houses, so almost every call is a single-family basement or crawlspace.

Lead lines are the homeowner's to replace

When the Village upgraded water meters between 2019 and 2021, it identified the probable service line material entering each building and mailed letters to properties with suspected or known lead. No letter means the Village does not suspect lead in your line. The Village's draft replacement plan and service line inventory are posted as of April 2026.

The whole service line is the homeowner's, and so is its repair. The Village notes lead lines are likelier in homes built before 1988. If you want the pipe looked at, our water service line repair visit identifies the material at the wall and prices the replacement before digging begins.

5

Lake Michigan water, bought and piped in the village

Lake Bluff buys its water from the Central Lake County Joint Action Water Agency, whose treatment plant sits inside the village along Route 176. Village Public Works pipes it through about 39 miles of mains and bills it. Disinfection is by chlorine, and the agency adds phosphate to keep lead and copper from leaching out of home plumbing.

The agency's own FAQ puts the water at about 8 grains per gallon of hardness. At that level mineral scale builds slowly inside tanks and on heating elements, so we look for sediment during a water heater replacement and discuss softening only where your fixtures and appliances show scale.

6

Permits and the Lake Bluff contractor license

The Village requires building permits for projects that alter or improve a property, and its permit guidelines list a water heater replacement as a permit item. Underground water, sewer and drainage pipes need an application with a drawing and a description. Every contractor working in the Village also needs a Village Contractor's License on top of the state plumbing license.

The inspection instructions on the Village's permit page are not consistent about who inspects, so we confirm with the Building Division before scheduling. Call 811 before any dig, and ask the Building Division at Village Hall, 40 E. Center Avenue, about your specific job.

Before We Arrive in Lake Bluff

Before We Arrive

1Shut the main

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops, then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the line.

2Hold the water back

Stop the dishwasher, washer and showers. A drain that is slow or gurgling cannot take more.

3Protect the pit

If the sump pump is running with water near its cord, leave the plug alone. Call and we will give you a safe next step.

4Lift valuables

Raise boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor, and photograph the damage for your insurer.

Lake Bluff at a glance

Local note

Village facts that explain the plumbing

Lake Bluff was incorporated in 1895 and covers about four square miles, with 5,616 residents in the 2020 Census. It adjoins North Chicago, Lake Forest, Green Oaks and the unincorporated Knollwood area, which is why crews from our base reach it quickly.

The lakefront has its own plumbing story. A regional sewage pumping station run by the reclamation district known as NSWRD sits at Sunrise Beach, and the water agency's primary intake is near Sunrise Beach too. Next door, Lake Forest and Waukegan are on our dispatch map as well.
