Plumber in Argonne Park and Argonne Heights, North Chicago, IL

Argonne Park and Argonne Heights are names that real estate sites use for residential blocks around Argonne Drive and Lewis Avenue in North Chicago. The homes range from early 1900s farmhouses and Cape Cods to ranches and split-levels, so a plumber should look at the actual pipes before guessing.

If water is coming through the floor or a drain is backing up, close the main water valve, keep people away from wet outlets, and call to talk through what you are seeing.

1

How this area is described

The names come from listing sites, so this page leans on streets instead of boundaries.

Homes.com, Redfin and other listing sites use the names Argonne Park and Argonne Heights for adjoining parts of North Chicago, and Homes.com places Argonne Park near Martin Luther King Jr. Drive, Lewis Avenue and Argonne Drive. The city's own pages describe places by street. City Hall, for example, is at 1850 Lewis Avenue.

Nearby blocks carry other listing names too, such as Dugdale Park and Lewis Glenn, and the sites do not agree on where one ends and the next begins. This page does not draw a map. If you are unsure whether your street counts, the address is all a plumber needs.

2

What the housing looks like

These figures come from a listing-site neighborhood guide, so read them as rough.

Homes.com describes farmhouses and Cape Cods from the late 1800s or early 1900s alongside ranch-style and split-level homes. It gives a median build year of 1957 and an average single-family size near 1,485 square feet. The same guide says owners and renters are about equal in number.

Homes from that span were built under older plumbing practice, and many have had pieces replaced since. That does not mean any particular house has a particular problem. The reliable way to know is to check what the supply and drain lines are made of, which a plumber can see at the meter, in the basement and at the cleanout.

Four things a plumber can check on an older house

  • ✓Supply pipe material. Copper, galvanized steel, plastic or a mix. It shows where the pipe enters the basement and at the meter.
  • ✓Main drain material. Cast iron, clay or plastic. A camera inspection shows what runs under the slab or the yard.
  • ✓Water heater age. The manufacture date is on the rating label, and the age helps decide between repair and replacement.
  • ✓Shutoff valves. The main valve and each fixture valve should close fully. A valve that will not turn is better found before an emergency.
3

Flooding and the city's stormwater basin

A city project at 20th Street and Lewis Avenue is the clearest local fact about basement water here.

A May 2026 Lake County News-Sun story, reposted on the city's website, says homes and buildings near 20th Street and Lewis Avenue have flooded when it rains for decades. It says homes built in the 1930s through the 1950s were not designed with water flow in mind, and that rainfall has increased since.

The story describes a detention basin about 10 feet deep next to Neal Math & Science Academy that should drain within about 24 hours after rain. Construction began in May with completion expected by early November 2026. It is part of a 55.7 million dollar infrastructure effort, and the story also describes a separate reconstruction of Argonne Drive.

The story does not say which neighborhood name covers that intersection, and a basin helps surrounding drainage, not every basement. It also does not repair a failed sump pump or a cracked floor drain inside a house. If a basement takes on water, check the pump, its discharge and the floor drain.

4

Plumbing work people in this area ask about

Basement water

A pump that will not run or a pit that fills too fast is sump pump repair work. Seepage through walls is a different problem, covered under basement waterproofing.

Water heaters

A tank that leaks, rumbles or runs out of hot water quickly may need water heater replacement.

Drains and main lines

Several fixtures gurgling or backing up at once points to the main line. Start with drain cleaning and ask about a camera look if it returns.

Hidden leaks

A damp spot or a rising water bill with no visible leak calls for leak detection.

Digging and street work near Argonne Drive

The same story describes the reconstruction of Argonne Drive as a project of about 23.2 million dollars, paid mostly by a federal grant. Street projects can change what is visible and marked near the curb. Anyone digging in Illinois should call 811 first so buried utilities are located, and that includes a water service line repair.

Renting here? Who to call first

Because owners and renters are about equal in number according to Homes.com, many calls start with a tenant. Tell the landlord about the problem in writing, close the nearest valve, and photograph any damage. A plumber can find the cause, and the owner decides about the repair.
