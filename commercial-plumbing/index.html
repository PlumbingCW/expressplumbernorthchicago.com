When a business loses water or floods overnight

A commercial plumbing emergency is any failure that stops you from operating or threatens the building: a burst line, a main drain that backs into the floor, a water heater leaking into stock, or no water at all. We treat those as same-day calls, and we answer after hours when the building is empty and a leak runs unnoticed.

First steps for the person on site

  • 1Shut the building's main water valve if water is spreading, and tell us if it will not close.
  • 2Turn off power to wet areas at the panel only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  • 3Move stock, files and electronics off the floor.
  • 4Photograph the water and the damage before cleanup for your insurer.
  • 5Call us with the address, the contact on site and how to get in after hours.

Who we work for in North Chicago

Of North Chicago's roughly 7,900 housing units, 2,229 are in apartment buildings with five or more units. For the owners and managers of those apartment properties, plumbing problems are commercial problems: many tenants, shared stacks, shared meters and a long chain of people to notify.

The city's own lead line plan notes that city water also serves non-residential customers, so a shop, clinic or office here is typically on the same municipal system as the house down the block. Where a property sits on the Navy's separate system, the rules differ, and we ask early.

Our office is at 3100 Skokie Highway, so a call from an address along Skokie Highway is a short trip for the crew. Crews also head west to Libertyville for business calls.

3

Hidden leaks in commercial buildings, honestly

Callers often have a symptom, not a diagnosis: a meter spinning after closing, a stain on a ceiling tile, a damp wall behind a counter. Finding the source can take real time in a building with several floors or tenants, and we explain what each stage involves before we start.

We begin with isolation. We shut sections of the system off one at a time and watch the meter, which narrows a leak without opening a wall. Our leak detection service adds acoustic and camera tools when isolation alone does not find it.

A straight word about scope

Commercial leak hunts are the jobs where expectations and scope drift apart most often. Before we start, we tell you how much of the building we can reach, what we cannot see, and what a failed search would still tell you. If the leak is behind a wall that belongs to a tenant or landlord, we say who must authorize the opening.

4

Floor drains, grease and lift pumps

Floor drains in kitchens, back rooms and mechanical spaces back up when grease, mop debris or lint builds up. A slow floor drain is the early warning, and a grated drain that overflows after hours is a real emergency. We clear it, tell you what came out, and suggest a schedule.

For repeating stoppages, hydro jetting scours the line instead of punching a hole through it. Lift pumps and ejector pits that serve a lower-level restroom are a different repair: if they fail, wastewater has nowhere to go, and we treat it as urgent.

When the blockage is on the building's main drain, our sewer line cleaning crew takes it from there.

5

Flood risk along the Skokie Highway corridor

Local note

A documented flood corridor

The county's flood review for the North Branch Chicago River watershed records road closures and damaged buildings where the Skokie River meets Route 41, blamed on culverts that are too small. FEMA money is paying to swap out small storm sewers near Berwyn and Bittersweet and to rework a Skokie River culvert, and the project names Skokie Highway as a place it should make safer.

If your building sits in or near that corridor, ask us to check floor drains, backwater protection and sump or ejector pits before the next heavy rain. Fixing the street does not protect the inside of your building, and that part is still on you and on us.

6

Backflow testing and code questions

Many commercial buildings have backflow preventers on their water supply, and the water utility decides which ones need regular testing. We do not guess at that requirement. Ask the North Chicago water department what applies to your account, and tell us what they say so we can work to it.

Permits for tenant work, new lines and fixture changes are handled by the building department's permit desk, 847-596-8650. Its page names the Illinois Plumbing Code in the adopted list, and the state health department (IDPH) issues plumbing licenses. We are licensed and insured, and we ask about permits before pricing a build-out through our plumbing installation service.
