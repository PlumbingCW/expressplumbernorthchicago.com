Emergency Faucet Repair in North Chicago, IL

A faucet that sprays, a supply line that has let go under the sink, or a tub valve that will not shut off cannot sit until Monday. We take calls 24/7. Open the cabinet and turn the small valve on each supply line clockwise. If nothing stops the flow, the main shutoff is next, and the dispatcher will talk you to it.

We repair kitchen and bathroom faucets, tub and shower valves, hose bibs and the supply lines that feed them. Where a repair costs nearly as much as a new fixture, you will hear that before we start.

1

When a faucet problem cannot wait

A steady drip can be scheduled. These cannot.

  • ✓A supply line or valve is spraying.. Closing the stop valve usually ends it. If the valve itself is leaking or will not turn, the main shutoff is the next move.
  • ✓A tub or shower valve will not shut off.. Hot water running through a wall is scalding risk and a ceiling problem for whoever lives below.
  • ✓Water is running behind or under a vanity.. Even a slow stream rots the cabinet floor and can reach the room underneath.
  • ✓A hose bib has split.. Outdoor spigots and the pipe behind them can crack when water freezes inside, and a split valve pushes water into the wall once the weather warms.

Anything outside this list we are happy to schedule for the first available slot. A same-day appointment is often possible when the day allows.

2

Find the faucet type, then the fix

Single-handle cartridge

The most common modern type. A worn cartridge or its seals causes drips and loose handles. Replacement is quick when the exact part is available.

Two-handle compression

The washer and seat wear down. These are simple to rebuild and often worth saving in older sinks.

Ceramic disc

Smooth quarter-turn handles. They fail rarely, but sediment can scratch the discs and cause drips.

Ball and kitchen pull-down

More small parts, more seals. Spray head hoses can crack where they connect to the faucet body.

Tub and shower valve

Behind the wall. A leaking handle or no temperature control often traces to the cartridge inside, which we can reach from the handle side.

Outdoor hose bib

Freeze damage and worn washers. We check for a leaking stem and a broken inner valve.

Bring us the brand if you know it. If you do not, we will identify the cartridge before ordering a part, because the wrong match wastes your time and ours.

3

The shutoff valve under your sink might be the real problem

In North Chicago about 32 percent of housing was built before 1960, and almost 19 percent before 1940. Under the sinks of those homes you often find valves that have not been closed in decades. Turn one and it can start leaking at the stem or refuse to seal, so a ten-minute faucet repair becomes a valve replacement.

We test the stop valves before we shut them for the repair. If a valve is stiff or green with corrosion, we tell you first. Replacing it gives you a working shutoff for the next emergency. That is also why we do not ask you to close old valves yourself if you have never tried them: forcing a corroded stem can cause the leak you were trying to avoid.

If several fixtures have worn valves and the supply pipes look tired, the larger question is repiping, which we explain on its own page.

4

Hard water guesses and what the city actually publishes

Local note

City water, no hardness figure

The city draws its tap water from Lake Michigan and treats it at its own plant, yet the reports we reviewed list sodium and fluoride but no hardness figure. So we do not blame mineral buildup on your aerator until we have looked at it.

What we can do is check the parts: crusty aerators, scaled cartridges and stained spouts tell us what your water is doing at your tap. If scale keeps returning, our water softener and filtration page covers what treatment might help, and a test is where that starts.

Low flow from a single faucet is almost always a clogged aerator or a partly closed stop valve. Low pressure everywhere points elsewhere, and we would look at the water service line or main valve.
