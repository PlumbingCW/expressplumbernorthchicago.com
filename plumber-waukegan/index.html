What Waukegan homes actually call about

The housing mix here pushes a short list of problems to the top.

The 2024 Census estimate counts 34,440 housing units in Waukegan, with a median build year of 1967. About 38 percent went up before 1960 and 17 percent date to 1939 or earlier. Homes of that age commonly carry cast iron drain stacks, galvanized supply runs and old clay or iron sewer laterals, so sewer work, leaking supply pipes and tired water heaters lead our Waukegan call sheet.

The stock is also unusually mixed. Close to half the units are detached houses, but more than 5,000 sit in buildings of two to four units and more than 6,000 in buildings of 10 to 49 units. In those buildings a leak often starts in one apartment and shows up in the one below, so we trace the source before anyone opens a wall.

Sewer backups and the city's private sewer permit

Waukegan collects the sewage in its own mains and the North Shore Water Reclamation District treats it. Your side of the arrangement is the private line from the building to the city main.

The city's sewer page says permits are required for cleanouts, sewer repairs and replacement work on private sewer lines. The application goes in online before work starts, and the pipe must be inspected before it is covered. Work started without a permit costs double in fees.

That shapes how we handle a sewer line repair here. We confirm the permit path first, have the utilities located through 811 before any digging, and leave the trench open until the inspection is done. If a camera run shows roots or a collapsed section, you get a clear price before work starts.

Flooding near the Waukegan River and basement water

Lake County's lakefront watershed plan counted 106 flood problem areas touching 185 properties in Waukegan after the July 2017 storm, the highest total for any municipality it lists. The same plan documents a September 2019 flash flood along the Waukegan River near Helmholz Avenue and notes that the 1938 flood overwhelmed the city's storm sewers. If your basement sits near that creek corridor, the pump in the pit matters more than any cosmetic fix.

Signs a sump pump is losing the fight

  • ✓Constant cycling. The pump runs in dry weather because the float or the check valve is failing.
  • ✓Slow drawdown. Water in the pit barely drops after a pump cycle.
  • ✓Grinding or humming. The motor is working but the impeller is jammed or worn.
  • ✓No backup source. A storm that floods the street often takes the power too, so ask about a battery backup.

We service and replace pumps through our sump pump repair work, and we say plainly when the real fix is grading or gutters rather than plumbing.

Water heater swaps need a permit in Waukegan

The city's plumbing page states that permits are required for all plumbing revisions and for the replacement of hot water heaters, along with exterior water lines out to the city b-box. Contractors must register with the city. We walk you through the permit step before the old tank leaves the basement, so the finished job does not leave an open permit behind. Ask about it when you book a water heater replacement.

A tank leaking at the base is finished. Shut the cold supply above it, turn off the gas or the breaker, and call. In an apartment building, tell dispatch which unit and which floor, because a dead heater there is often a shared-system question.

A dead heater at night is still a same-day problem when a slot is open, and dispatch answers around the clock.

Lead, mains and the city water plant

Waukegan pumps its own water from Lake Michigan through about 230 miles of mains. The city plant opened in 1928, and new filters on its west bay were finished in 2019 and 2020. The city says lead is not present in its treated water, which makes any lead question a question about the private service line between the main and your basement wall.

The city runs a Lead Service Line Replacement Program with an address lookup and a service line material survey. When we are in the basement we look at the pipe where it enters and tell you what it is. If it needs work, a service line replacement can be planned alongside that program rather than around it.

Before We Arrive in Waukegan

Before We Arrive

1Find the shutoff

The main valve is usually inside the basement where the line enters. The city's curb stop near the street is called the b-box, so leave that one to the city.

2Rest the drains

If a floor drain is rising, skip the dishwasher, the laundry and every flush. Each gallon you add goes to the same low spot.

3Mind the electrics

Water near a panel, an outlet or a sump pump means stay out. Cut the breaker only if you can reach it standing dry.

4Name the unit

In a multi-unit building, tell dispatch which unit has the water and who holds a key to the one below.
