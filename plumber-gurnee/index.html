Gurnee homes are young, and their first appliances are aging out

Gurnee's median home was built in 1992, and about 79 percent of its 12,081 housing units date to 1980 or later. The biggest single decade is the 1990s, with 4,746 homes, or about 39 percent. Moderately hard water, and what it does to a tank

The Village says its water is moderately hard, about 8 grains per gallon or 137 milligrams per liter as calcium carbonate. The water is Lake Michigan water bought wholesale from CLC/JAWA, the regional lake water agency, finished with chlorine as the disinfectant. At that hardness, scale builds up on heating elements and the tank floor over the years.

Hard-water signs we see in Gurnee kitchens and basements

  • ✓Popping or rumbling tanks. Sediment on the bottom traps heat and makes the tank noisy.
  • ✓Slow hot water recovery. A scaled element or burner takes longer to heat the same volume.
  • ✓White crust on fixtures. Spots and crusts on faucets and showerheads point to mineral content.
  • ✓Clogged tankless units. Scale narrows the heat exchanger, so a tankless water heater needs regular descaling.

If scale is your main complaint, we can talk through water softener and filtration options, and we will say so if the symptom is something else.

The Des Plaines River corridor and a failed sump pump

The Village names the low-lying area around the Des Plaines River and its tributaries as its most dangerous flood hazard. In July 2017 the river gage at Gurnee crested at 12.09 feet, above the 11.95 feet recorded in 1986. Photos from that event show water near Route 132 and Old Grand Avenue. The Village has acquired a structure off Old Grand Avenue and, in a 2013 grant application, proposed buying two on Kilbourne Road.

For homes away from the river, the Village's own warning is simple: a gravity sewer backup or a sump pump failure will likely lead to basement flooding, and residents are encouraged to install backup systems. We install and repair pumps under sump pump repair. Work inside the mapped flood hazard area needs a Watershed Development Permit from the Village.

Lead and galvanized lines: the Village says none are known

The Village's water report states there are no known lead or galvanized service lines in Gurnee. Public Works keeps a service line material inventory and can be reached at its Kilbourne Road facility. That is good news, and it means a Gurnee water service call is more likely to be a leak, a pressure problem or a failed shutoff than a pipe replacement for lead.

We still look at the line where it enters during any visit, because an inventory is only as good as the record behind it.

Heater permits and the Village's registered plumber rule

In Gurnee a water heater job runs through the Building Safety Division. The Village's water heater page says the licensed plumber must be registered with the Village before the permit is released, and that a written Letter of Intent must come from the plumber. The letter goes on company letterhead, states the address and scope, is signed by the plumber of record and is notarized or carries a corporate seal.

The Village does not state a permit rule for sewer repairs on the pages we reviewed, so we ask the Building Safety Division before we start. Anyone digging in Illinois should call 811 first, and plumbers in this state are licensed by the health department, IDPH.

Before We Arrive in Gurnee

Before We Arrive

1Cut the heater

Turn the heater's gas control to pilot or off, or switch off its breaker, then close the cold water valve above it.

2Close the main

Turn the main valve fully clockwise, then open an upstairs faucet to let the pipes drain.

3Lift the stored things

Move boxes, carpet and electronics out of the wet area before the water spreads.

4Skip the drain cleaners

Chemical openers can burn a plumber's skin and fittings, so leave a slow drain for us.

Gurnee facts that matter on a repair

Local note

Two sewer bills, one village

Gurnee had 30,706 residents in the 2020 Census over 13.5 square miles. The Village maintains the sewer collection lines, and sanitary treatment happens at the regional district's own plant in Gurnee, known as NSWRD. East of the tollway, customers get a separate bill from that district, while west of it a Lake County Public Works processing fee appears on the Village bill.

That split is a billing detail, not a repair rule. A blockage on your side of the property line is yours either way. If you are comparing towns, Waukegan and Libertyville are both covered from our North Chicago base.
