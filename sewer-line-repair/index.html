Emergency Sewer Line Repair in North Chicago, IL

Sewage coming up through a floor drain or several fixtures backing up at once is an emergency, and North Chicago Express Plumbing answers 24/7. While you wait, stop using water in the house, keep people and pets away from the affected area, and do not run the washer or flush.

We find out what is wrong inside the line with a camera before we recommend digging. Plenty of backups end with a cleaning. When the pipe itself has failed, we show you the footage.

1

Signs your sewer line has failed, not just clogged

A clog can be cleared and forgotten. A damaged sewer line keeps causing trouble. Warning signs include repeated backups after cleaning, gurgling from more than one drain, sewage smell in the yard, a sunken patch of lawn over the line, and slow drains in the lowest bathroom or basement.

When the line has a crack, an offset joint, a belly that holds water, or roots that grew through a joint, cleaning only buys time. We use a camera to see which problem you have and record the footage so you can see it too.

2

When a sewer backup is an emergency

Sewage coming up through a floor drain, tub, or toilet is a same-day problem. Water in a basement after a storm adds a second question: is it sewage from the sanitary line, groundwater from outside, or both? Tell us what you saw and when it started.

After-hours calls are normal for us. We work out of 3100 Skokie Highway and can dispatch at night, and a same-day visit is the goal for a backup. Do not pour chemicals into a backed-up line, because they can burn whoever opens it next.

3

How North Chicago's sewer setup affects your repair

The North Shore Water Reclamation District serves North Chicago, and the district states that sanitary and storm sewers in its service area are separate systems with no combined sewers. That means storm water is not supposed to enter the sanitary line. Rain-linked backups are worth investigating for a cracked lateral or a bad connection on the private side.

Who owns the sewer main versus the private lateral depends on the exact spot. The published sources are not clear on who maintains local collector sewers, so we tell you to ask the district or the city's public works office before you pay for a repair in the street.

4

Flood history and why basements back up here

Lake County's flood study ties North Chicago to the July 2017 storm, which brought three to seven inches of rain across the area. A survey found ten flood problem areas and 23 affected properties in the city, including three sewer backups. The Skokie Highway corridor has a record of flooding where undersized culverts met heavy rain.

A FEMA-funded storm sewer project is replacing undersized storm sewers at Berwyn and Bittersweet Avenues and improving the Skokie River channel. That work targets street flooding, not your private line. A cracked lateral or a failed backwater valve on your property still needs a fix from you. We check these during a camera run.

5

What a sewer repair visit looks like

  1. 1

    Stop the damage

    We find the cleanout, clear enough of the line to relieve the backup, and tell you what to do with affected rooms.

  2. 2

    Camera the line

    A camera shows the pipe condition, any offsets, roots, or breaks, and measures how far the problem sits from the cleanout.

  3. 3

    Price the options

    You get a price for each path, spot repair, trenchless lining where it fits, or replacement.

  4. 4

    Call 811 and permit

    JULIE marks buried utilities before we dig. The permit question goes to the city's inspection staff, since rules differ by address.

  5. 5

    Repair, test, and restore

    We repair the line, camera it again or flow-test it, and backfill the work area.

If you want a record before deciding, ask about a standalone sewer camera inspection. If the line is only blocked, sewer line cleaning may be all you need, and heavy grease may call for hydro jetting.
