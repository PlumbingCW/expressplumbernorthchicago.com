Emergency Water Heater Replacement in North Chicago, IL

A tank that is leaking onto the floor or a heater that has gone cold is an urgent problem, and North Chicago Express Plumbing takes these calls 24/7. While you wait for us, close the cold-water valve on top of the tank, switch the gas control to pilot or the breaker off, and put towels or a wet vac under the leak.

Most of our replacement calls end the same day when a matching unit is available. We tell you the price for the whole job before we cut a single pipe.

1

When a water heater failure is an emergency

Some failures can wait until morning. These cannot.

Water pooling around the base of a tank usually means the steel shell has rusted through from the inside. No part can fix that, and the leak tends to grow. A heater that drips from the relief valve pipe is a different story and sometimes only needs a valve or a pressure fix, so tell us exactly where the water shows up.

Treat a flooded basement utility closet, a scorched smell near the burner, or a tank that rumbles and hisses loudly as same-day problems. If a rental unit has no hot water at all and the weather is cold, that is also urgent. After-hours calls get the same dispatch as daytime calls.

Do this while you wait

  • 1Shut the cold-water supply valve at the top of the tank.
  • 2Turn the gas control to pilot, or switch off the breaker for an electric unit.
  • 3Open one hot faucet upstairs so the tank does not hold pressure.
  • 4Move anything that can soak up water away from the floor drain.
  • 5If you smell gas, walk outside first and phone the gas utility or 911.
2

Replace it or repair it?

We would rather fix a cheap part than sell you a new tank. A bad thermostat, a failed heating element, or a worn thermocouple is a repair, and our water heater repair page covers those. Replacement makes sense when the tank itself leaks, when a repair would cost close to a new unit, or when the heater is old enough that the next part will fail soon.

We check the data plate for the build date, look at the flue and the gas line, and drain a little water from the bottom valve to see how much sediment has settled. You get a plain answer and a price for both options when both are realistic.

3

What a replacement visit looks like

  1. 1

    Look and quote

    We confirm the fuel type, tank size, venting, and the space around the old unit, then give you a clear price before work starts.

  2. 2

    Drain and disconnect

    We shut off fuel and water, drain the tank to a floor drain or hose, and disconnect the old supply lines, flue, and gas connector.

  3. 3

    Set the new unit

    We set the heater level, fit a new relief valve and discharge pipe, and replace worn connectors instead of reusing them.

  4. 4

    Fire it up and test

    We check for gas leaks at every joint, confirm the burner or elements run, and test the flue draft before we call it done.

  5. 5

    Haul away

    We carry out the old tank and show you where the shutoffs are so you know them next time.

Ask the city's building inspection office whether your address needs a plumbing permit for the swap. The city's own page does not spell out a water heater permit process, so we will not guess for you.

5

What North Chicago housing does to a water heater

The median North Chicago home was built in 1973, but 32.3% of the housing stock dates from before 1960, and about 18.7% from before 1940. Older buildings often have tight utility closets, aging flues, and gas lines that were sized for appliances from another era. We look at all three before we quote, because a new tank that fits the old hole in the wall can still vent badly.

Your water comes from the city's own treatment plant, which draws from Lake Michigan and uses chlorine as its disinfectant. The city's water reports do not publish a hardness number, so we do not assume one. We judge by what comes out of the drain valve, and we tell you what we see in the sediment.

6

Renters, landlords, and apartment buildings

About two out of three North Chicago households rent, and roughly 28% of housing units sit in buildings with five or more units. That changes who calls and how the job runs. A tenant should report a leak or cold water to the landlord or property manager right away and keep the date and time. A manager can call us directly and approve the work.

In a multi-unit building, tell us whether the heater serves one unit or the whole building. A shared system may involve a boiler or recirculation loop, and our boiler repair work may be the better fit. Photos of the data plate help us bring the right unit on the first trip.
