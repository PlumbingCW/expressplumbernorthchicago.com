Water Heater Installation in North Chicago, IL

A new tank water heater has to match three things: the people using it, the fuel feeding it, and the vent or circuit behind it. We install gas and electric tank units in North Chicago houses, rentals and small apartment buildings, and you get a clear price before work starts.

If the old tank is leaking right now, close the cold supply valve on the pipe above it and call. Urgent leaks are handled 24/7. Planned swaps usually fit a same-day appointment when available.

How an install day runs, start to finish

  1. 1

    Check the fuel, the space and the vent

    Before anything is ordered we look at the gas line or electric circuit, the flue path, the floor drain and how much room the tank has. Surprises here are what turn a simple swap into a long day.

  2. 2

    Drain and disconnect the old tank

    We shut off the water, drain the tank, and take apart the old connections. Corroded shutoff valves get replaced rather than reused.

  3. 3

    Set and connect the new unit

    The new tank goes on a level base with fresh water connections, a relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, and a vent or electrical hookup that matches its fuel.

  4. 4

    Fire it up and test

    We check gas joints, confirm the flue draws, run hot water at a fixture, and show you where the shutoffs and the temperature setting are.

2

Sizing for a house, a rental unit or a six-flat

Only 37.5% of the homes in North Chicago are single-family detached houses. The rest are attached homes, duplexes, small walk-ups and larger complexes, and 28.2% of all units sit in buildings with five or more units. About two-thirds of households rent.

That mix matters because one tank rarely fits every setup. A one-bath apartment, a four-bedroom house and a building with a shared boiler room each need a different answer, and a landlord replacing a tank usually wants the same size back with less downtime for tenants. We size by household and fixtures, not by what the last tank happened to be.

3

Gas, electric and the vent question

A gas tank burns fuel and has to push exhaust out of the building. If the vent is cracked, undersized or pitched wrong, exhaust can spill into the room. North Chicago's building department lists the 2021 fuel gas and mechanical codes among the codes it has adopted, so vent routing and clearances are part of what we confirm, not an afterthought.

Electric tanks skip the flue but need the right breaker and wire gauge. If the panel is already full, say so when you call. Switching fuel is possible, but it means new gas piping or a new circuit, and the price changes with it.

If you smell gas

Leave the building first. Call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches, light anything or return until the utility says it is safe.

4

Permits, licensing and who may do the work

Local note

Ask the city before the old tank comes out

The building department page for North Chicago does not describe a plumbing permit process for water heater swaps, so the safe move is to ask the Inspection and Permit office at City Hall, 1850 Lewis Avenue, before work starts. Rules can differ for rentals and multi-unit buildings.

In Illinois, plumbers are licensed by the Illinois Department of Public Health, and you can look up a license on the state's verification page. Ask any plumber for the license and check it yourself.

5

Same tank again, a different size, or tankless?

Most swaps go back to a tank, because it is the simplest fit for an existing closet or basement corner. If you want endless hot water and have the gas supply for it, read about a tankless water heater, which needs a larger gas line and different venting.

If your tank is only misbehaving, a water heater repair may cost less than a swap. A pilot that will not stay lit or a tank that has stopped heating can be a failed part. A tank that is leaking from the shell cannot be fixed, and that case belongs on the water heater replacement page. Sediment rumbling and rusty hot water are signs the tank is near the end.

Signs a tank is past repairing

  • ✓Water pooling under the tank from the shell, not from a fitting
  • ✓Rust-colored hot water that clears up when you run the cold tap
  • ✓A tank old enough that you cannot find its date on the label
  • ✓Repeated repair calls within a single year

What changes the price of a new tank

What moves the price

A straight like-for-like swap costs less than a job that changes fuel, size or venting. These are the factors we weigh in your price.

Tank size and efficiencyLarger tanks and higher-efficiency models cost more up front and sometimes less to run.
Fuel and ventingA gas tank with a new flue costs more than an electric tank on an existing circuit.
Condition of old connectionsSeized valves, corroded fittings and failed shutoffs add parts and time.
AccessA tank in a tight closet or down steep basement stairs takes longer to move.
Code itemsExpansion tanks, discharge piping and a pan under the unit are added where the setup calls for them.
If a leak damaged your home, your insurer may cover part of the water damage, but the tank itself is usually your cost. Ask your carrier before you decide.

Getting the space ready

Before We Arrive

1Clear three feet around the tank

Move boxes, paint cans and laundry baskets so the crew can reach the unit and the shutoffs.

2Photograph the data label

The label shows size, fuel and model. A photo helps us arrive with the right parts.

3Tell tenants or neighbors

If the building shares a water line, warn others that the water will be off for a while.

4Know where the main shutoff is

If you cannot find it, tell us on the phone and we will help you locate it.
