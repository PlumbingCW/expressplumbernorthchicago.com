Plumber in Downtown North Chicago, IL

Downtown North Chicago is a district of older storefronts, offices and apartments along Sheridan Road, not a subdivision of houses. A single building here can hold a shop on the ground floor and homes above it, so one leak can involve two tenants and an owner.

If water is spreading, close the main shutoff for the building or the unit, keep people out of wet areas near electrical outlets, and call to talk through what you are seeing.

1

Where the downtown district sits

The location comes from a published planning study, so this page does not draw a boundary of its own.

A 2008 station-area planning study for North Chicago describes Sheridan Road between 14th Street and Broadway Avenue as the city's historic downtown district. The study was built around the North Chicago Metra station, which sits at 1633 Lakeside Avenue on the Union Pacific North Line.

That study is almost two decades old, so treat its description as a starting point. Storefronts, vacancies and apartments change over time, and the study did not say which units were occupied. If you are calling about a particular building, the street address matters more than the district name.

2

A mixed-use district, not a subdivision

The study describes the buildings, and those descriptions shape the plumbing questions worth asking.

The study describes mixed-use and commercial buildings along Sheridan Road, some with residential units above the storefronts, plus a four-story building that houses seniors and people with disabilities. It also notes vacant buildings and vacant lots. None of this says anything about pipe materials or past problems, so this page does not guess at them.

Those are general questions, not findings about any building on Sheridan Road. A plumber who looks at the actual pipes can answer them for your address.

3

Plumbing work people in this district ask about

Business plumbing

Shops, offices and restaurants need work planned around opening hours. See commercial plumbing for how that is handled.

Drains and shared lines

A slow floor drain or a backed-up line in a shared building starts with drain cleaning. If it keeps returning, a sewer camera inspection shows what the pipe looks like inside.

Leaks between floors

When a stain appears under a unit, leak detection finds the source before anyone opens a wall.

Water service line

The pipe that carries water from the street to the building is a water service line repair job.

Describe the building, the unit and the problem, and a plumber can tell you what to check first. Call (847) 281-8626.

4

What to do in the first ten minutes of a leak

  1. 1

    Find the nearest shutoff

    Close the valve for the unit if there is one. If not, close the building's main valve and tell the owner or manager.

  2. 2

    Move what water can ruin

    Lift stock, records and electronics off the floor and away from the wet wall or ceiling.

  3. 3

    Stay clear of electricity

    Do not stand in water near outlets, panels or lit fixtures. Turn the power off at the panel only if you can reach it dry.

  4. 4

    Photograph and call

    Take photos of the damage and the source, then call to describe what you see. Photos help with insurance and with the plumber's diagnosis.

5

Permits and who to call at the city

The city's building department lists a plumber registration application among its permit forms and says it has adopted the Illinois Plumbing Code. Its inspection and permit line is 847-596-8650. The page does not spell out which jobs need a permit, so call before work on a building starts.

Water service questions go to the city water department at 847-596-8634, and Public Works is at 847-596-8870. In its lead service line program the city treats a service line in two parts. The city handles the line from the water main to the property line, and the owner handles the rest up to the building.

6

The city's lead service line program

North Chicago is replacing lead water service lines at no cost to eligible owners in one defined phase of about 175 properties, east of Green Bay Road, south of Argonne Road and north of Broadway Avenue. An Illinois EPA program pays for it, and the city says it cannot replace a line without the owner's participation.

That is a city project area, not a downtown program. Call the water department to ask whether an address is covered. Anyone who digs for a service line repair should call 811 first so buried utilities are marked.
