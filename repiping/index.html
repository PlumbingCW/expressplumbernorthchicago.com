Repiping in North Chicago, IL

Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes inside your walls, floors and ceilings. It fits homes where galvanized steel has rusted shut, copper keeps springing pinhole leaks, or the water runs brown and weak. We repipe North Chicago houses, flats and small buildings in planned stages.

This is a planned project, with same-day appointments when available for the first look. If a pipe is spraying now, shut the main water valve and call. Urgent leaks are handled 24/7.

1

Signs the pipes in your walls are wearing out

Pipes rarely fail without warning. Water pressure fades over months. A shower that used to be strong now trickles. Hot water takes longer to arrive, or the faucet spits rust-colored water for a minute after the house sits idle.

Leaks are the other clue. One pinhole in a copper line can be fixed with a burst pipe repair visit. Three in the same year usually mean the whole run is thinning from the inside. Stains on a ceiling or a damp spot on a basement wall are worth a call before the drywall goes soft, and if the source is unclear we can start with leak detection.

Repipe warning signs

  • ✓Rust or brown tint in the water, mostly in the morning
  • ✓Low pressure at several fixtures, not just one
  • ✓Pinhole leaks or green crust on copper joints
  • ✓Visible rust, flaking or swelling on steel pipe
  • ✓A repair that failed again close to the first fix
2

Galvanized steel and why it fails from the inside

Galvanized pipe is steel with a zinc coating. The coating wears away, rust builds on the inner wall, and the opening narrows until little water gets through. A house with galvanized supply lines can have good pressure at the street and poor pressure at the tub.

Cutting into galvanized pipe also shows how bad it is. If we find the opening mostly closed, a spot repair buys little, and the next joint tends to fail soon after. In that case we show you the cut section and talk through a repipe in stages.

3

Your service line, and the pipes behind it

Local note

North Chicago's lead line inventory

The city's own lead service line plan lists 5,653 service lines. Of those, 849 are lead and another 144 are galvanized lines marked for replacement. A free replacement phase covers eligible properties east of Green Bay Road, south of Argonne Road and north of Broadway Avenue.

That program deals with the pipe from the water main to your house. The pipes inside your home are a separate matter, and a repipe is the way to replace them. If you are in the city's program area, ask about the service line first and plan your interior repipe around it. See our water service line repair page for the outside pipe.

4

Copper, PEX or a mix: choosing the new pipe

Both copper and PEX are used for home supply lines. Copper is rigid and long-lasting, and has a long track record in homes. PEX is flexible plastic, easier to run through finished walls, and does not corrode from the inside the way old steel does.

Copper versus PEX for a repipe
FactorCopperPEX
Walls and ceilingsMore cutting, more patchingSnakes through with fewer openings
JointsSoldered, each one a skill pointCrimped or expanded fittings
Cold climatesCan burst when frozenTolerates some freezing but should still be protected
Water chemistryPinholes in aggressive waterNot subject to the same corrosion
CostUsually higherUsually lower

We discuss which one suits your house, and sometimes the answer is copper in the basement and PEX upstairs.

5

How a repipe works in a home you are living in

  1. 1

    Survey and plan

    We trace the existing lines, count fixtures and mark where new pipe will run.

  2. 2

    Rough in the new lines

    New pipe goes in beside the old, through basements, crawl spaces and the smallest wall openings we can manage.

  3. 3

    Switch over

    We shut water, connect fixtures to the new lines, and pressure-test before closing anything.

  4. 4

    Remove or abandon the old pipe

    Old lines that can come out easily do. Others are capped and left in place where removal would harm the structure.

  5. 5

    Patch and review

    We close access openings and walk you through the work. Finish painting is yours, and we say so in advance.
