Emergency Hydro Jetting in North Chicago, IL

When a drain keeps backing up even after it has been snaked, high-pressure water is often the next step, and North Chicago Express Plumbing runs hydro jetting 24/7 for urgent blockages. Until we arrive, stop using the affected fixtures, keep the floor drain clear of belongings, and do not add chemical cleaner.

A jetter scrubs the whole inside of the pipe, not just a hole through the clog. We still check the pipe with a camera first, because jetting is the wrong choice for some failing lines.

1

What hydro jetting does that a cable cannot

A drain cable cuts or hooks through a blockage and leaves a channel. The coating on the pipe wall stays in place and the line narrows again. A jetter feeds a hose with a special nozzle into the pipe and sprays water at high pressure in several directions, stripping grease, scale, and soft debris off the wall.

That is why it fits lines that have been cleared before and slowed down again. Heavy grease from a kitchen line and mineral buildup in an older pipe are the classic cases. Fine roots can be cut with the right nozzle, though large root masses may need a cutter first.

2

Urgent jetting situations

Some blockages cannot wait for a scheduled visit.

A main line that is backing up into a lower unit, a kitchen line that has stopped a restaurant or a shared household kitchen, and a floor drain that overflows each time someone uses a bathroom above are urgent. We take calls at any hour and offer same-day service when a jetting unit and technician are free.

If water is already on the floor, stay out of it and keep children away. Tell us how many fixtures are affected and where the lowest drain is. That helps us decide whether to jet through a cleanout or a roof vent.

3

When we do not jet

High-pressure water is not right for every pipe. Cracked, collapsed, or badly offset lines can be made worse. Very old clay or thin cast iron may need a gentler method or a repair. Joints that are already loose can wash out.

That is why we run a camera before we jet when the pipe's condition is unknown. If we see damage, we explain it and move to sewer line repair. If the line is sound, jetting goes ahead and you see before and after footage.

4

Buildings in North Chicago that tend to need it

Roughly 2,229 North Chicago housing units are in buildings with five or more units, and about 815 of those are in buildings with twenty or more. Shared stacks and long horizontal drains in larger buildings collect grease and wipes from many households. A single cable pass may clear one apartment and leave the shared line coated.

Kitchens in mixed-use buildings and shared household kitchens add cooking grease to that load. Tell us the building type and the number of units when you call, so we bring the right hose length.

5

How a jetting visit works

  1. 1

    Camera first

    We look at the pipe and confirm it is sound enough for pressure.

  2. 2

    Set up access

    We use a cleanout, a pulled fixture, or a roof vent, and protect nearby surfaces.

  3. 3

    Jet the line

    We run the hose in stages, working from the far end of the clog back toward the cleanout.

  4. 4

    Check the result

    We camera or flow-test the line again so you can see the pipe wall is clean.

  5. 5

    Advise a schedule

    For a grease-heavy line, we may suggest a routine cleaning interval.

What changes the price of hydro jetting

What changes the price

Jetting is equipment-heavy work. The price follows the size of the job and how hard the pipe is to reach.

Pipe size and lengthA four-inch branch and a long main run need different hose lengths and time.
Severity of buildupHeavy grease or scale takes several passes.
Access pointA cleanout is simple. A roof vent or a removed fixture adds labor.
Camera workInspection before and after is part of a responsible jetting job.
TimingRush calls and nights are priced apart from booked visits, and you hear that up front.
Maintenance cleaning is rarely covered. Damage from a sudden backup might be, so keep photos and our written report.
